How DNA Digital Data Storage Works

1. Writing (DNA Chemical Synthesis)

Any digital file (photos, music, text) is composed of 0s and 1s. We group these bits into pairs (00, 01, 10, 11) and map them to DNA's four chemical nucleotides: Adenine (A), Cytosine (C), Guanine (G), and Thymine (T). Liquid synthesizers assemble physical DNA strands molecule by molecule.

2. Archival Storage (Liquid Pool or Silica)

The synthesized DNA strands are stored in a test tube suspension or encapsulated inside micro-spheres of synthetic silica (glass). At room temperature, silica-encapsulated DNA remains stable for thousands of years with zero electrical power consumed.

3. Random Access (PCR Amplification)

Each file is synthesized with a unique 20-base primer tag. To retrieve a specific file from billions of mixed files in the tube, matching primers are added, and Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR) exponentially amplifies only that targeted file.

4. Reading & Decoding (Sequencing)

Next-generation sequencers (like Oxford Nanopore or Illumina) read the order of bases in the DNA molecule. Software converts the bases back into binary bits ($A \to 00$, $C \to 01$, $G \to 10$, $T \to 11$), bit-perfectly reconstructing the original file!
